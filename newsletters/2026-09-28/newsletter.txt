EDITOR'S TAKE
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Late-stage pipeline expansion into progressive multiple sclerosis takes center
stage as Zenas BioPharma initiates Phase 3 recruitment for its CNS-penetrable
BTK inhibitor, orelabrutinib, across both non-active SPMS and PPMS cohorts.
This double-pronged push underscores an industry-wide prioritization of BTK
inhibition to address neurodegeneration, a theme further echoed by Roche's
fenebrutinib trial advancing to active, non-recruiting status in relapsing MS.
Concurrently, the anti-CD20 standard of care faces impending margin pressure
as Amgen progresses its Phase 3 trial for ABP 692, a proposed biosimilar to
Roche's market-leading Ocrevus. With foundational paradigm shifts being
evaluated alongside Johns Hopkins' newly completed TREAT-MS trial on early
aggressive therapy, the market's focus now shifts to whether novel mechanisms
like orelabrutinib can successfully unlock efficacy in progressive disease
states where traditional anti-inflammatory agents fall short.

1. A Study of Orelabrutinib in Patients With Secondary Progressive Multiple
Sclerosis
   Impact 4/5 | ClinicalTrials.gov | 23 Sep 2026 | Phase 3 | Recruiting |
   Zenas BioPharma (USA), LLC | NCT07299019

   Zenas BioPharma is recruiting for a global Phase 3, randomized, double-
   blind study (NCT07299019) evaluating orelabrutinib against placebo in
   approximately 990 patients with non-active secondary progressive multiple
   sclerosis. Orelabrutinib is a CNS-penetrable Bruton's tyrosine kinase (BTK)
   inhibitor, and patients will be randomized 2:1 to receive treatment for 24
   to 60 months with a minimum duration of 12 months.

   WHY IT MATTERS: Zenas BioPharma's initiation of a pivotal Phase 3 trial for
   orelabrutinib advances a novel CNS-penetrant BTK inhibitor into non-active
   SPMS, directly challenging competitors developing late-stage BTK inhibitors
   for progressive MS populations.

   Impact 4/5 | ClinicalTrials.gov | 22 Sep 2026 | Phase 3 | Active Not

   Amgen has advanced its Phase 3 trial (NCT06700343) evaluating its proposed
   biosimilar, ABP 692, into the Active, Not Recruiting stage. The study's

   https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT06700343

3. A Study of Orelabrutinib in Patients With Primary Progressive Multiple
Sclerosis
   Impact 4/5 | ClinicalTrials.gov | 22 Sep 2026 | Phase 3 | Recruiting |
   Zenas BioPharma (USA), LLC | NCT07067463

   Zenas BioPharma is recruiting approximately 705 subjects for a global Phase
   3, randomized, double-blind study evaluating its CNS-penetrable BTK
   inhibitor, orelabrutinib, against placebo in Primary Progressive Multiple
   Sclerosis (PPMS). Patients will undergo 2:1 randomized treatment for 30 to
   60 months with a 12-month minimum duration (NCT07067463).

   WHY IT MATTERS: The progression of orelabrutinib into Phase 3 PPMS
   development directly impacts Roche, which holds the only currently approved
   disease-modifying therapy for PPMS with Ocrevus, as well as rival BTKi
   developers in late-stage non-relapsing MS trials.
   https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT07067463

   subjects with relapsing-remitting multiple sclerosis. The trial evaluated

   https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT06083753

5. A Study to Investigate the Efficacy of Fenebrutinib in Relapsing Multiple
Sclerosis (RMS)
   Impact 3/5 | ClinicalTrials.gov | 25 Sep 2026 | Phase 2 | Active Not
   Recruiting | Hoffmann-La Roche | NCT05119569

   Hoffmann-La Roche's Phase 2 trial (NCT05119569) evaluating fenebrutinib in
   participants with relapsing multiple sclerosis (RMS) is currently active,
   not recruiting. The placebo-controlled study assesses the effect of the BTK
   inhibitor on brain MRI parameters, safety, and pharmacokinetics across a
   double-blind treatment phase and an optional open-label extension. The
   primary target completion date for the trial is September 25, 2026.

   WHY IT MATTERS: Completion of this Phase 2 study will provide critical MRI
   efficacy and safety data for Roche's non-covalent BTK inhibitor
   fenebrutinib, informing its competitive positioning against rival BTK
   inhibitors from Sanofi and Merck KGaA in relapsing MS.
   https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT05119569

6. Traditional Versus Early Aggressive Therapy for Multiple Sclerosis Trial
   Impact 3/5 | ClinicalTrials.gov | 23 Sep 2026 | N/A | Completed | Johns
   Hopkins University | NCT03500328

   Johns Hopkins University has completed the pragmatic, randomized controlled
   TREAT-MS trial (NCT03500328). The study evaluated whether an early
   aggressive treatment strategy versus a traditional first-line therapy
   approach reduces intermediate-term disability risk in higher- and lower-
   risk relapsing MS patients. Additionally, it assessed outcomes in lower-
   risk patients with breakthrough disease who switched to high-efficacy
   versus alternative first-line therapies.

   WHY IT MATTERS: Data comparing early aggressive intervention against
   escalation models could shift physician prescribing paradigms toward high-
   efficacy DMTs earlier in the relapsing MS treatment sequence.
   https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT03500328
