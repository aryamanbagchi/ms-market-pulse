Editor's Take

Late-stage pipeline expansion into progressive multiple sclerosis takes center stage as Zenas BioPharma initiates Phase 3 recruitment for its CNS-penetrable BTK inhibitor, orelabrutinib, across both non-active SPMS and PPMS cohorts. This double-pronged push underscores an industry-wide prioritization of BTK inhibition to address neurodegeneration, a theme further echoed by Roche's fenebrutinib trial advancing to active, non-recruiting status in relapsing MS. Concurrently, the anti-CD20 standard of care faces impending margin pressure as Amgen progresses its Phase 3 trial for ABP 692, a proposed biosimilar to Roche's market-leading Ocrevus. With foundational paradigm shifts being evaluated alongside Johns Hopkins' newly completed TREAT-MS trial on early aggressive therapy, the market's focus now shifts to whether novel mechanisms like orelabrutinib can successfully unlock efficacy in progressive disease states where traditional anti-inflammatory agents fall short.

A Study of Orelabrutinib in Patients With Secondary Progressive Multiple Sclerosis IMPACT 4/5
ClinicalTrials.gov · 23 Sep 2026 · Phase 3 · Recruiting · Zenas BioPharma (USA), LLC · NCT07299019

Zenas BioPharma is recruiting for a global Phase 3, randomized, double-blind study (NCT07299019) evaluating orelabrutinib against placebo in approximately 990 patients with non-active secondary progressive multiple sclerosis. Orelabrutinib is a CNS-penetrable Bruton's tyrosine kinase (BTK) inhibitor, and patients will be randomized 2:1 to receive treatment for 24 to 60 months with a minimum duration of 12 months.

Why it matters
Zenas BioPharma's initiation of a pivotal Phase 3 trial for orelabrutinib advances a novel CNS-penetrant BTK inhibitor into non-active SPMS, directly challenging competitors developing late-stage BTK inhibitors for progressive MS populations.

Comparison Between ABP 692 and Ocrevus® (Ocrelizumab) IMPACT 4/5
ClinicalTrials.gov · 22 Sep 2026 · Phase 3 · Active Not Recruiting · Amgen · NCT06700343

Amgen has advanced its Phase 3 trial (NCT06700343) evaluating its proposed biosimilar, ABP 692, into the Active, Not Recruiting stage. The study's primary objectives are to demonstrate pharmacokinetic similarity between ABP 692 and both US- and EU-sourced Ocrevus (ocrelizumab).

Why it matters
Progress in this Phase 3 trial directly threatens Roche's Ocrevus franchise by accelerating biosimilar competition for high-efficacy anti-CD20 therapy in multiple sclerosis.

A Study of Orelabrutinib in Patients With Primary Progressive Multiple Sclerosis IMPACT 4/5
ClinicalTrials.gov · 22 Sep 2026 · Phase 3 · Recruiting · Zenas BioPharma (USA), LLC · NCT07067463

Zenas BioPharma is recruiting approximately 705 subjects for a global Phase 3, randomized, double-blind study evaluating its CNS-penetrable BTK inhibitor, orelabrutinib, against placebo in Primary Progressive Multiple Sclerosis (PPMS). Patients will undergo 2:1 randomized treatment for 30 to 60 months with a 12-month minimum duration (NCT07067463).

Why it matters
The progression of orelabrutinib into Phase 3 PPMS development directly impacts Roche, which holds the only currently approved disease-modifying therapy for PPMS with Ocrevus, as well as rival BTKi developers in late-stage non-relapsing MS trials.

A Study to Investigate the Efficacy of Fenebrutinib in Relapsing Multiple Sclerosis (RMS) IMPACT 3/5
ClinicalTrials.gov · 25 Sep 2026 · Phase 2 · Active Not Recruiting · Hoffmann-La Roche · NCT05119569

Hoffmann-La Roche's Phase 2 trial (NCT05119569) evaluating fenebrutinib in participants with relapsing multiple sclerosis (RMS) is currently active, not recruiting. The placebo-controlled study assesses the effect of the BTK inhibitor on brain MRI parameters, safety, and pharmacokinetics across a double-blind treatment phase and an optional open-label extension. The primary target completion date for the trial is September 25, 2026.

Why it matters
Completion of this Phase 2 study will provide critical MRI efficacy and safety data for Roche's non-covalent BTK inhibitor fenebrutinib, informing its competitive positioning against rival BTK inhibitors from Sanofi and Merck KGaA in relapsing MS.

Traditional Versus Early Aggressive Therapy for Multiple Sclerosis Trial IMPACT 3/5
ClinicalTrials.gov · 23 Sep 2026 · N/A · Completed · Johns Hopkins University · NCT03500328

Johns Hopkins University has completed the pragmatic, randomized controlled TREAT-MS trial (NCT03500328). The study evaluated whether an early aggressive treatment strategy versus a traditional first-line therapy approach reduces intermediate-term disability risk in higher- and lower-risk relapsing MS patients. Additionally, it assessed outcomes in lower-risk patients with breakthrough disease who switched to high-efficacy versus alternative first-line therapies.

Why it matters
Data comparing early aggressive intervention against escalation models could shift physician prescribing paradigms toward high-efficacy DMTs earlier in the relapsing MS treatment sequence.
