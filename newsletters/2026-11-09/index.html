Editor's Take

Zenas BioPharma has significantly escalated the competitive pressure in progressive multiple sclerosis by initiating twin global Phase 3 trials for its CNS-penetrable BTK inhibitor, orelabrutinib. These pivotal studies targeting non-active secondary progressive MS (NCT07299019) and primary progressive MS (NCT07067463) highlight an industry-wide pivot toward addressing non-relapsing forms of the disease where unmet need remains highest. Meanwhile, Biogen faces commercial headwinds as regulatory watchdogs investigate allegations that the drugmaker restricted access to a critical MS diagnostic test. Together, these events underscore how the market is shifting simultaneously on two fronts: clinical innovation moving deeper into progressive disease, and heightened scrutiny over commercial access strategies. Moving forward, strategy teams must track whether orelabrutinib's CNS penetration translates into superior long-term disability outcomes in its multi-year placebo-controlled trials.

A Study of Orelabrutinib in Patients With Secondary Progressive Multiple Sclerosis IMPACT 4/5
ClinicalTrials.gov · 04 Nov 2026 · Phase 3 · Recruiting · Zenas BioPharma (USA), LLC · NCT07299019

Zenas BioPharma has initiated recruitment for a global Phase 3 randomized, double-blind study evaluating orelabrutinib in non-active secondary progressive multiple sclerosis (spMS). The trial (NCT07299019) will enroll approximately 990 patients randomized 2:1 to the CNS-penetrable BTK inhibitor or placebo for 24 to 60 months, with a minimum treatment duration of 12 months.

Why it matters
Zenas BioPharma is advancing orelabrutinib into pivotal trials for non-active spMS, challenging established MS players developing BTK inhibitors in progressive populations where therapeutic options remain severely limited.

A Study of Orelabrutinib in Patients With Primary Progressive Multiple Sclerosis IMPACT 4/5
ClinicalTrials.gov · 03 Nov 2026 · Phase 3 · Recruiting · Zenas BioPharma (USA), LLC · NCT07067463

Zenas BioPharma has initiated recruitment for a global Phase 3, randomized, double-blind study (NCT07067463) evaluating its CNS-penetrable BTK inhibitor, orelabrutinib, in patients with primary progressive multiple sclerosis (PPMS). The trial aims to enroll approximately 705 subjects randomized 2:1 against placebo, with treatment duration ranging from 30 to 60 months and a 12-month minimum exposure.

Why it matters
This trial advances a novel CNS-penetrant BTK inhibitor into pivotal development for PPMS, intensifying competition against established and pipeline progressive MS disease-modifying therapies.
