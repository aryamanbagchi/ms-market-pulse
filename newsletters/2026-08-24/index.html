Editor's Take

Zenas BioPharma’s aggressive Phase 3 expansion of orelabrutinib into progressive MS populations marks the week's most consequential strategic move, signaling a push to capture non-active secondary progressive and primary progressive indications where therapeutic options remain severely limited. This push reinforces a broader industry pattern centered on late-stage BTK inhibitor progression, evidenced by Novartis reaching active status in its Phase 3 REMODEL-1 trial for remibrutinib and Roche advancing Phase 2 trials for fenebrutinib. Concurrently, TG Therapeutics is fortifying its anti-CD20 franchise by advancing multiple clinical trials for ublituximab, targeting subcutaneous delivery via autoinjector, modified dosing regimens, and pediatric populations to rival established blockbusters. Meanwhile, Novartis is defending its market share through a new observational study directly comparing the real-world effectiveness and economic burden of Kesimpta against Roche's Ocrevus. Moving forward, stakeholders should monitor whether upcoming late-stage data readouts for these oral BTK inhibitors can demonstrate sufficient efficacy in progressive forms of MS to successfully challenge the dominant anti-CD20 paradigm.

A Study to Evaluate the Pharmacokinetics, Pharmacodynamics, Safety, Radiological and Clinical Effects of Subcutaneous Ublituximab in Participants With Relapsing Multiple Sclerosis (RMS) IMPACT 4/5
ClinicalTrials.gov · 21 Aug 2026 · Phase 3 · Active Not Recruiting · TG Therapeutics, Inc. · NCT07211633

TG Therapeutics has progressed its Phase 3 trial (NCT07211633) evaluating a subcutaneous (SC) formulation of ublituximab in participants with relapsing multiple sclerosis (RMS) to active, not recruiting status. The study is designed to assess the pharmacokinetics, pharmacodynamics, safety, radiological, and clinical effects of SC ublituximab compared to the currently approved intravenous (IV) administration. The primary objective centers on establishing PK comparability between the SC and IV delivery routes in RMS patients.

Why it matters
A successful SC formulation of ublituximab would allow TG Therapeutics to compete directly with subcutaneous anti-CD20 options, lowering administration burden and defending market share against incumbents like Novartis's Ofatumumab and Roche's subcutaneous Ocrelizumab.
