Editor's Take

Novartis suffered a major pipeline setback as weak relapse data for its oral BTK inhibitor remibrutinib disappointed expectations, disrupting the company's growth strategy in relapsing MS. This vulnerability accelerates a broader shift in the BTK inhibitor landscape, where Zenas BioPharma is aggressively advancing orelabrutinib into primary and secondary progressive MS with two global Phase 3 initiations, while Roche prepares for an impending FDA decision on fenebrutinib. Meanwhile, safety liabilities came into focus for high-potency modalities following Bristol Myers Squibb's report of a patient death in its CAR T MS trial and emerging data linking anti-CD20 therapies to inflammatory bowel disease. Meanwhile, TG Therapeutics continues to fortify its anti-CD20 franchise by progressing subcutaneous ublituximab through Phase 3 trials to challenge established intravenous options. Strategic attention now centers on Roche's upcoming regulatory decision and early safety reporting from high-intensity cell therapy trials to determine how market share will reallocate.

Study to Evaluate Safety, Efficacy and Pharmacokinetics (PK) of a Modified Regimen of Ublituximab IMPACT 4/5
ClinicalTrials.gov · 23 Oct 2026 · Phase 3 · Recruiting · TG Therapeutics, Inc. · NCT05877963

TG Therapeutics is recruiting for a Phase 3b study (NCT05877963) evaluating a modified regimen of ublituximab in relapsing multiple sclerosis (RMS). Part A (open-label) and Part C (open-label, evaluating participants with suboptimal prior anti-CD20 experience) measure efficacy via T1 Gd-enhancing lesions, while Part B is a randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled pharmacokinetics assessment. Why it matters
Successful optimization of ublituximab's dosing schedule and proof of efficacy in anti-CD20 suboptimal responders could strengthen TG Therapeutics' market positioning against anti-CD20 competitors Roche (ocrelizumab) and Novartis (ofatumumab).
