EDITOR'S TAKE
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Zenas BioPharma stole the spotlight this week by advancing its CNS-penetrable
BTK inhibitor, orelabrutinib, directly into two Phase 3 trials targeting
primary progressive and non-active secondary progressive multiple sclerosis.
This aggressive move highlights a broader clinical push into underserved
progressive and pediatric MS populations, moving past traditional relapsing-
remitting paradigms. Parallel developments reinforce this focus, with Roche
initiating a Phase 2 trial for its own BTK inhibitor, fenebrutinib, in
pediatric relapsing MS, and Bristol-Myers Squibb recruiting for a Phase 3
trial comparing ozanimod to fingolimod in young patients. Meanwhile, Quantum
BioPharma secured FDA IND clearance for Lucid-MS to enter Phase 2, and Queen
Mary University of London continues to evaluate cladribine in advanced, high-
EDSS populations. Strategy teams should now monitor whether Zenas's bold 2:1
placebo-controlled trials in progressive MS can establish BTK inhibition as
the definitive standard for halting non-relapsing disease progression.

1. A Study of Orelabrutinib in Patients With Primary Progressive Multiple
Sclerosis
   Impact 4/5 | ClinicalTrials.gov | 10 Sep 2026 | Phase 3 | Recruiting |
   Zenas BioPharma (USA), LLC | NCT07067463

   Zenas BioPharma has initiated recruiting for a global Phase 3 randomized,
   double-blind study evaluating its CNS-penetrable BTK inhibitor,
   orelabrutinib, against placebo in approximately 705 patients with primary
   progressive multiple sclerosis (PPMS). Participants will receive treatment
   in a 2:1 ratio for 30 to 60 months, with a minimum required duration of 12
   months. The multicenter trial (NCT07067463) will assess the efficacy and
   safety of the pipeline asset in this non-relapsing population.

   WHY IT MATTERS: The initiation of a Phase 3 trial for orelabrutinib in PPMS
   expands late-stage competition in the BTK inhibitor class against
   incumbents like Roche, Sanofi, Novartis, and Merck KGaA targeting
   progressive MS indications.
   https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT07067463

2. A Study of Orelabrutinib in Patients With Secondary Progressive Multiple
Sclerosis
   Impact 4/5 | ClinicalTrials.gov | 10 Sep 2026 | Phase 3 | Recruiting |
   Zenas BioPharma (USA), LLC | NCT07299019

   Zenas BioPharma has initiated a global Phase 3, randomized, double-blind
   study (NCT07299019) evaluating its CNS-penetrable BTK inhibitor,
   orelabrutinib, in patients with non-active secondary progressive multiple
   sclerosis. The trial plans to recruit approximately 990 subjects globally,
   randomizing them 2:1 against placebo for a treatment period ranging between
   24 and 60 months.

   WHY IT MATTERS: The initiation of Phase 3 testing for orelabrutinib
   introduces a new competitive BTK inhibitor into clinical development
   targeting non-active SPMS, challenging established MS players advancing
   therapies in progressive disease populations.
   https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT07299019

3. Study to Evaluate the Effectiveness and Safety of Ozanimod Compared to
Fingolimod in Children and Adolescents With Relapsing Remitting Multiple
Sclerosis
   Impact 4/5 | ClinicalTrials.gov | 10 Sep 2026 | Phase 3 | Recruiting |
   Bristol-Myers Squibb | NCT06408259

   Bristol-Myers Squibb is recruiting for a Phase 3 study evaluating the
   effectiveness, safety, tolerability, drug levels, and drug effects of
   ozanimod compared to fingolimod. The trial focuses on pediatric patients,
   specifically children and adolescents diagnosed with relapsing remitting
   multiple sclerosis (RRMS).

   WHY IT MATTERS: Bristol-Myers Squibb's Phase 3 head-to-head trial aims to
   position ozanimod as a pediatric RRMS option directly competing against
   Novartis's established fingolimod in this younger patient demographic.
   https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT06408259

4. A Pharmacokinetics (PK), Pharmacodynamics (PD), Safety and Tolerability
Study of Fenebrutinib in Children and Adolescents With Relapsing Multiple
Sclerosis (RMS)
   Impact 3/5 | ClinicalTrials.gov | 10 Sep 2026 | Phase 2 | Recruiting |
   Hoffmann-La Roche | NCT07161258

   Hoffmann-La Roche has initiated recruitment for an open-label, single-arm
   Phase 2 trial (NCT07161258) evaluating fenebrutinib in pediatric patients
   with relapsing multiple sclerosis aged 10 to under 18 years. The study
   assesses the pharmacokinetics, pharmacodynamics, safety, and tolerability
   of the asset across a Dose Exploration Period followed by an Optional
   Extension Period.

   WHY IT MATTERS: Roche's extension of its BTK inhibitor fenebrutinib into
   pediatric RMS positions the asset to potentially compete in younger patient
   demographics currently targeted by established disease-modifying therapies.
   https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT07161258

5. ChariotMS - Cladribine to Halt Deterioration in People With Advanced
Multiple Sclerosis
   Impact 3/5 | ClinicalTrials.gov | 08 Sep 2026 | Phase 2, Phase 3 | Active
   Not Recruiting | Queen Mary University of London | NCT04695080

   Queen Mary University of London is running ChariotMS, an active, non-
   recruiting Phase 2b multi-centre, randomised, double-blind, placebo-
   controlled trial evaluating cladribine tablets (MAVENCLAD) in advanced
   multiple sclerosis (pwAMS). The trial evaluates a 3.5mg/kg dose over 24
   months to test safety, efficacy, cost-effectiveness, and mechanism of
   action in patients with an EDSS of 6.5 to 8.5. Cladribine is not currently
   used in this advanced population, which relies on wheelchair assistance or
   experiences severe disability.

   WHY IT MATTERS: Demonstrating clinical benefit and cost-effectiveness of
   oral cladribine in patients with EDSS 6.5-8.5 could expand Merck KGaA's
   addressable market into advanced MS populations currently lacking approved
   disease-modifying therapies.
   https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT04695080
