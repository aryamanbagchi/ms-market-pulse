Editor's Take

Zenas BioPharma stole the spotlight this week by advancing its CNS-penetrable BTK inhibitor, orelabrutinib, directly into two Phase 3 trials targeting primary progressive and non-active secondary progressive multiple sclerosis. This aggressive move highlights a broader clinical push into underserved progressive and pediatric MS populations, moving past traditional relapsing-remitting paradigms. Parallel developments reinforce this focus, with Roche initiating a Phase 2 trial for its own BTK inhibitor, fenebrutinib, in pediatric relapsing MS, and Bristol-Myers Squibb recruiting for a Phase 3 trial comparing ozanimod to fingolimod in young patients. Meanwhile, Quantum BioPharma secured FDA IND clearance for Lucid-MS to enter Phase 2, and Queen Mary University of London continues to evaluate cladribine in advanced, high-EDSS populations. Strategy teams should now monitor whether Zenas's bold 2:1 placebo-controlled trials in progressive MS can establish BTK inhibition as the definitive standard for halting non-relapsing disease progression.

A Study of Orelabrutinib in Patients With Primary Progressive Multiple Sclerosis IMPACT 4/5
ClinicalTrials.gov · 10 Sep 2026 · Phase 3 · Recruiting · Zenas BioPharma (USA), LLC · NCT07067463

Zenas BioPharma has initiated recruiting for a global Phase 3 randomized, double-blind study evaluating its CNS-penetrable BTK inhibitor, orelabrutinib, against placebo in approximately 705 patients with primary progressive multiple sclerosis (PPMS). Participants will receive treatment in a 2:1 ratio for 30 to 60 months, with a minimum required duration of 12 months. The multicenter trial (NCT07067463) will assess the efficacy and safety of the pipeline asset in this non-relapsing population.

Why it matters
The initiation of a Phase 3 trial for orelabrutinib in PPMS expands late-stage competition in the BTK inhibitor class against incumbents like Roche, Sanofi, Novartis, and Merck KGaA targeting progressive MS indications.

A Study of Orelabrutinib in Patients With Secondary Progressive Multiple Sclerosis IMPACT 4/5
ClinicalTrials.gov · 10 Sep 2026 · Phase 3 · Recruiting · Zenas BioPharma (USA), LLC · NCT07299019

Zenas BioPharma has initiated a global Phase 3, randomized, double-blind study (NCT07299019) evaluating its CNS-penetrable BTK inhibitor, orelabrutinib, in patients with non-active secondary progressive multiple sclerosis. The trial plans to recruit approximately 990 subjects globally, randomizing them 2:1 against placebo for a treatment period ranging between 24 and 60 months.

Why it matters
The initiation of Phase 3 testing for orelabrutinib introduces a new competitive BTK inhibitor into clinical development targeting non-active SPMS, challenging established MS players advancing therapies in progressive disease populations.

ClinicalTrials.gov · 10 Sep 2026 · Phase 2 · Recruiting · Hoffmann-La Roche · NCT07161258

Hoffmann-La Roche has initiated recruitment for an open-label, single-arm Phase 2 trial (NCT07161258) evaluating fenebrutinib in pediatric patients with relapsing multiple sclerosis aged 10 to under 18 years. ChariotMS - Cladribine to Halt Deterioration in People With Advanced Multiple Sclerosis IMPACT 3/5
ClinicalTrials.gov · 08 Sep 2026 · Phase 2, Phase 3 · Active Not Recruiting · Queen Mary University of London · NCT04695080

Queen Mary University of London is running ChariotMS, an active, non-recruiting Phase 2b multi-centre, randomised, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial evaluating cladribine tablets (MAVENCLAD) in advanced multiple sclerosis (pwAMS). The trial evaluates a 3.5mg/kg dose over 24 months to test safety, efficacy, cost-effectiveness, and mechanism of action in patients with an EDSS of 6.5 to 8.5. Cladribine is not currently used in this advanced population, which relies on wheelchair assistance or experiences severe disability.

Why it matters
Demonstrating clinical benefit and cost-effectiveness of oral cladribine in patients with EDSS 6.5-8.5 could expand Merck KGaA's addressable market into advanced MS populations currently lacking approved disease-modifying therapies.
