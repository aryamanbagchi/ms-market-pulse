Editor's Take

Zenas BioPharma took center stage this week by initiating a Phase 3 trial for orelabrutinib in non-active secondary progressive multiple sclerosis, directly targeting an underserved patient segment where high-efficacy options remain sparse. This movement highlights an industry-wide pivot toward aggressive clinical intervention in progressive forms of the disease, further evidenced by Roche launching a Phase 2 trial for RO7268489 as an add-on therapy to market-leading ocrelizumab. Roche is simultaneously laying groundwork in relapsing MS with a new Phase 1 study for subcutaneous RO7845860, signaling a dual effort to shield its core franchise while expanding into progressive paradigms. Meanwhile, secondary studies continue to explore biomarker-guided strategies like serum neurofilament and advanced PET imaging to better assess neurodegeneration. Strategic watchpoints now focus on trial execution speed for orelabrutinib and whether Roche's combination approach can deliver meaningful efficacy signals in progressive populations.

A Study of Orelabrutinib in Patients With Secondary Progressive Multiple Sclerosis IMPACT 4/5
ClinicalTrials.gov · 07 Oct 2026 · Phase 3 · Recruiting · Zenas BioPharma (USA), LLC · NCT07299019

Zenas BioPharma has initiated a Phase 3 randomized, double-blind, global trial (NCT07299019) evaluating its CNS-penetrable BTK inhibitor, orelabrutinib, in patients with non-active Secondary Progressive Multiple Sclerosis (spMS). The multicenter study is currently recruiting approximately 990 participants randomized 2:1 against placebo, with treatment duration spanning 24 to 60 months.

Why it matters
Zenas BioPharma's progression into Phase 3 for non-active spMS advances a CNS-penetrant BTK inhibitor into an underserved progressive MS population, increasing competition for rivals developing late-stage neurodegenerative MS pipelines.

The National Institute of Allergy and Infectious Diseases (NIAID) is recruiting for TRAP-MS (NCT03109288), an exploratory Phase 1/2 trial evaluating four monotherapies and combinations in adult progressive multiple sclerosis patients. The study uses lumbar punctures and blood tests to measure cerebrospinal fluid and systemic biomarkers of inflammation over up to six years to determine if biomarker changes can predict therapeutic response. Cladribine Tablets as an Exit Therapy Strategy IMPACT 2/5
ClinicalTrials.gov · 06 Oct 2026 · Observational · Completed · University Hospital, Strasbourg, France · NCT06887426

University Hospital, Strasbourg has completed an observational study (NCT06887426) evaluating cladribine tablets (CladT) as an exit therapy strategy in aging relapsing-remitting MS patients aged 45 and older who were disease-stable for over 5 years on prior DMTs. Using dynamic propensity score matching on cohort databases from France, Belgium, and Switzerland, the study measured disease stability over a minimum 24-month follow-up against treatment continuation or discontinuation. Key endpoints assessed the proportion of patients free from relapses, 6-month confirmed EDSS progression, and new MRI activity.

Why it matters
Demonstrating cladribine's effectiveness as a finite exit therapy could inform de-escalation strategies in older, stable RRMS patients, impacting long-term retention strategies for continuous DMT manufacturers like Merck KGaA, Novartis, and Roche.
