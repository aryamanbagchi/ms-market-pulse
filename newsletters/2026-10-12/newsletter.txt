EDITOR'S TAKE
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Zenas BioPharma took center stage this week by initiating a Phase 3 trial for
orelabrutinib in non-active secondary progressive multiple sclerosis, directly
targeting an underserved patient segment where high-efficacy options remain
sparse. This movement highlights an industry-wide pivot toward aggressive
clinical intervention in progressive forms of the disease, further evidenced
by Roche launching a Phase 2 trial for RO7268489 as an add-on therapy to
market-leading ocrelizumab. Roche is simultaneously laying groundwork in
relapsing MS with a new Phase 1 study for subcutaneous RO7845860, signaling a
dual effort to shield its core franchise while expanding into progressive
paradigms. Meanwhile, secondary studies continue to explore biomarker-guided
strategies like serum neurofilament and advanced PET imaging to better assess
neurodegeneration. Strategic watchpoints now focus on trial execution speed
for orelabrutinib and whether Roche's combination approach can deliver
meaningful efficacy signals in progressive populations.

1. A Study of Orelabrutinib in Patients With Secondary Progressive Multiple
Sclerosis
   Impact 4/5 | ClinicalTrials.gov | 07 Oct 2026 | Phase 3 | Recruiting |
   Zenas BioPharma (USA), LLC | NCT07299019

   Zenas BioPharma has initiated a Phase 3 randomized, double-blind, global
   trial (NCT07299019) evaluating its CNS-penetrable BTK inhibitor,
   orelabrutinib, in patients with non-active Secondary Progressive Multiple
   Sclerosis (spMS). The multicenter study is currently recruiting
   approximately 990 participants randomized 2:1 against placebo, with
   treatment duration spanning 24 to 60 months.

   WHY IT MATTERS: Zenas BioPharma's progression into Phase 3 for non-active
   spMS advances a CNS-penetrant BTK inhibitor into an underserved progressive
   MS population, increasing competition for rivals developing late-stage
   neurodegenerative MS pipelines.
   https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT07299019

   The National Institute of Neurological Disorders and Stroke (NINDS) has
   completed a Phase 1 study (NCT02511028) evaluating ferumoxytol, an
   ultrasmall superparamagnetic iron oxide nanoparticle, on 7 Tesla brain MRI.

   inflammation in adults aged 18–70 with multiple sclerosis compared to

   WHY IT MATTERS: Successful validation of ferumoxytol on ultra-high-field 7T

   modifying therapies.
   https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT02511028

15. Cladribine Tablets as an Exit Therapy Strategy
   Impact 2/5 | ClinicalTrials.gov | 06 Oct 2026 | Observational | Completed |
   University Hospital, Strasbourg, France | NCT06887426

   University Hospital, Strasbourg has completed an observational study
   (NCT06887426) evaluating cladribine tablets (CladT) as an exit therapy
   strategy in aging relapsing-remitting MS patients aged 45 and older who
   were disease-stable for over 5 years on prior DMTs. Using dynamic
   propensity score matching on cohort databases from France, Belgium, and

   EDSS progression, and new MRI activity.

   WHY IT MATTERS: Demonstrating cladribine's effectiveness as a finite exit
   therapy could inform de-escalation strategies in older, stable RRMS
   patients, impacting long-term retention strategies for continuous DMT
   manufacturers like Merck KGaA, Novartis, and Roche.
   https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT06887426
