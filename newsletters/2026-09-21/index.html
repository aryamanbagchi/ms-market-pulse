Editor's Take

The Bruton's tyrosine kinase (BTK) inhibitor class reached critical clinical milestones this week, headlined by Hoffmann-La Roche transitioning its pivotal Phase 3 fenebrutinib trials in relapsing MS and primary progressive MS to active, non-recruiting status. This advancement underscores a broader industry push to address progressive forms of MS with novel oral mechanisms, further evidenced by Zenas BioPharma initiating Phase 3 recruitment for orelabrutinib in both primary progressive and non-active secondary progressive MS. Meanwhile, Sanofi is moving to secure route-of-administration flexibility for frexalimab, initiating a Phase 3 study comparing subcutaneous to intravenous dosing in RMS and non-relapsing SPMS. In parallel, non-pharmacologic interventions continue to benchmark the ceiling of efficacy, with NIAID actively enrolling its Phase 3 trial evaluating autologous hematopoietic stem cell transplant against best available therapy. Strategy teams should now monitor double-blind primary analysis disclosures for fenebrutinib, which will provide the first definitive readout on whether BTK inhibition can surpass established standard-of-care agents like ocrelizumab and teriflunomide.

Study to Evaluate the Efficacy and Safety of Fenebrutinib Compared With Teriflunomide in Relapsing Multiple Sclerosis (RMS) IMPACT 4/5
ClinicalTrials.gov · 17 Sep 2026 · Phase 3 · Active Not Recruiting · Hoffmann-La Roche · NCT04586023

Hoffmann-La Roche's Phase 3 NCT04586023 trial evaluating fenebrutinib versus teriflunomide in adult participants with relapsing multiple sclerosis has transitioned to active, not recruiting. The trial assesses the efficacy and safety of fenebrutinib on disability progression and relapse rate using a 1:1 randomization against teriflunomide. Initiation of an open-label extension phase will be decided by the sponsor following the disclosure of double-blind treatment results.

Why it matters
Completion of recruitment for Roche's Phase 3 fenebrutinib trial brings a key oral BTK inhibitor closer to pivotal data, threatening market share for active RMS controls like Sanofi's Aubagio (teriflunomide).

A Study to Evaluate the Efficacy and Safety of Fenebrutinib Compared With Ocrelizumab in Adult Participants With Primary Progressive Multiple Sclerosis IMPACT 4/5
ClinicalTrials.gov · 17 Sep 2026 · Phase 3 · Active Not Recruiting · Hoffmann-La Roche · NCT04544449

Hoffmann-La Roche's Phase 3 NCT04544449 trial comparing oral fenebrutinib against IV ocrelizumab in 985 adult participants with primary progressive multiple sclerosis (PPMS) has reached 'Active, Not Recruiting' status. The study evaluates efficacy and safety regarding disability progression, with eligible completers transitioning to an open-label extension phase pending positive primary analysis results. Participants discontinuing early will not be replaced.

Why it matters
This head-to-head Phase 3 study directly impacts Roche's PPMS franchise by positioning its investigational oral BTK inhibitor fenebrutinib against its own standard-of-care IV therapy ocrelizumab.

Best Available Therapy Versus Autologous Hematopoietic Stem Cell Transplant for Multiple Sclerosis (BEAT-MS) IMPACT 4/5
ClinicalTrials.gov · 16 Sep 2026 · Phase 3 · Recruiting · National Institute of Allergy and Infectious Diseases (NIAID) · NCT04047628

The NIAID-sponsored Phase 3 BEAT-MS trial (NCT04047628) is actively recruiting 156 participants to evaluate Autologous Hematopoietic Stem Cell Transplantation (AHSCT) against Best Available Therapy (BAT) in treatment-resistant relapsing MS. Participants are randomized 1:1 in this multi-center, prospective, rater-masked study and will be followed for 72 months post-randomization.

Why it matters
A positive Phase 3 readout for AHSCT over high-efficacy BATs could establish cellular therapy as a durable, non-pharmacological alternative for highly active relapsing MS, threatening long-term market share for leading DMT franchises across Roche, Novartis, Sanofi, and Biogen.

A Study of Orelabrutinib in Patients With Primary Progressive Multiple Sclerosis IMPACT 4/5
ClinicalTrials.gov · 15 Sep 2026 · Phase 3 · Recruiting · Zenas BioPharma (USA), LLC · NCT07067463

Zenas BioPharma has initiated a global, randomized, double-blind Phase 3 study (NCT07067463) evaluating its CNS-penetrable BTK inhibitor, orelabrutinib, versus placebo in approximately 705 patients with primary progressive multiple sclerosis (PPMS). Participants will undergo a 2:1 randomized treatment scheme for 30 to 60 months, with a minimum required duration of 12 months. The study is actively recruiting to evaluate the efficacy and safety of the candidate in this hard-to-treat population.

Why it matters
If successful, Zenas BioPharma's orelabrutinib could introduce a novel CNS-penetrant BTK inhibitor option to challenge Roche's Ocrevus in the underserved primary progressive MS segment.

A Study of Orelabrutinib in Patients With Secondary Progressive Multiple Sclerosis IMPACT 4/5
ClinicalTrials.gov · 15 Sep 2026 · Phase 3 · Recruiting · Zenas BioPharma (USA), LLC · NCT07299019

Zenas BioPharma is recruiting approximately 990 patients globally for a Phase 3, randomized, double-blind study evaluating its CNS-penetrable BTK inhibitor, orelabrutinib, against placebo in non-active secondary progressive multiple sclerosis (spMS). Subjects will be randomized 2:1 and treated for a duration ranging from 12 to 60 months. The study (NCT07299019) aims to evaluate the safety and efficacy of the asset in a high-unmet-need progressive MS population.

Why it matters
If successful, orelabrutinib could establish a new standard of care in non-active SPMS and intensify Phase 3 competition among BTK inhibitor developers including Sanofi, Roche, and Merck KGaA.

Non-inferiority Study of Frexalimab Subcutaneous Administration Compared to Intravenous Administration in Adult Participants With Multiple Sclerosis IMPACT 4/5
ClinicalTrials.gov · 15 Sep 2026 · Phase 3 · Recruiting · Sanofi · NCT07325292

Sanofi has initiated a Phase 3 randomized, open-label trial (NCT07325292) comparing subcutaneous (SC) to intravenous (IV) administration of frexalimab administered every 4 weeks. The 48-week study is actively recruiting adult patients aged 18 to 60 with relapsing multiple sclerosis (RMS) and non-relapsing secondary progressive multiple sclerosis (nrSPMS). The protocol includes an optional Part C extended treatment period and a 6-month safety follow-up for patients who discontinue treatment.

Why it matters
Sanofi is advancing a subcutaneous formulation of its anti-CD40L antibody frexalimab to potentially offer a more convenient delivery route than intravenous infusion for RMS and nrSPMS populations.
