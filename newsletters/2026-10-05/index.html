Editor's Take

Roche took center stage this week as the FDA accepted filing applications and granted Priority Review for its oral BTK inhibitor fenebrutinib across both relapsing and primary progressive MS, establishing a historic precedent for the drug class. This regulatory breakthrough highlights an industry-wide acceleration toward novel oral mechanisms and progressive indications, further evidenced by Zenas BioPharma initiating a Phase 3 study of its CNS-penetrable BTK inhibitor orelabrutinib in non-active secondary progressive MS. Concurrently, late-stage oral and subcutaneous options continue to advance, as Immunic AG transitioned its Phase 3 ENSURE-1 trial for vidofludimus calcium (IMU-838) to active status and Genentech initiated a Phase 4 switch study for subcutaneous ocrelizumab. Together, these developments signal a competitive re-alignment around targeted oral therapies and enhanced delivery methods across underserved progressive populations. Stakeholders should now watch for the FDA's accelerated PDUFA decision date for fenebrutinib and primary completion data from Immunic's ENSURE-1 trial.

Study to Evaluate the Efficacy, Safety, and Tolerability of IMU-838 in Patients With Relapsing Multiple Sclerosis IMPACT 4/5
ClinicalTrials.gov · 01 Oct 2026 · Phase 3 · Active Not Recruiting · Immunic AG · NCT05134441

Immunic AG's Phase 3 ENSURE-1 trial evaluating IMU-838 (vidofludimus calcium) versus placebo in adults with relapsing multiple sclerosis has transitioned to Active, Not Recruiting status. The multi-center, randomized, double-blinded study is assessing the efficacy, safety, and tolerability of the oral DHODH inhibitor in RMS patients. Primary completion will provide key pivotal data on IMU-838's performance against placebo in the relapsing MS segment.

Why it matters
Completion of enrollment in ENSURE-1 advances Immunic's oral DHODH inhibitor closer to pivotal readout, positioning it as a potential late-stage oral competitor to established relapsing MS therapies.

A Study of Orelabrutinib in Patients With Secondary Progressive Multiple Sclerosis IMPACT 4/5
ClinicalTrials.gov · 30 Sep 2026 · Phase 3 · Recruiting · Zenas BioPharma (USA), LLC · NCT07299019

Zenas BioPharma has initiated a global Phase 3 randomized, double-blind study (NCT07299019) evaluating its CNS-penetrable BTK inhibitor, orelabrutinib, in patients with non-active secondary progressive multiple sclerosis (spMS). The study is currently recruiting approximately 990 subjects globally, randomized 2:1 against placebo, with treatment duration ranging from 12 to 60 months. This trial expands Phase 3 evaluation of BTK inhibition specifically into the non-active spMS population.

Why it matters
If successful, Zenas BioPharma's orelabrutinib could establish a new standard of care in non-active spMS—an underserved segment with high unmet need—thereby directly competing against incumbent progressive MS therapies and rival BTK inhibitors in development by Sanofi, Roche, and Merck KGaA.

A Study of Ocrelizumab Administered Subcutaneously in Participants With Multiple Sclerosis Who Switch From an Approved Anti-CD20 Therapy IMPACT 3/5
ClinicalTrials.gov · 30 Sep 2026 · Phase 4 · Recruiting · Genentech, Inc. · NCT07609719

Genentech has initiated a Phase 4 study (NCT07609719) evaluating subcutaneous ocrelizumab co-formulated with recombinant human hyaluronidase (rHuPH20) in patients with relapsing multiple sclerosis or primary progressive multiple sclerosis. The trial is actively recruiting participants switching from previously approved anti-CD20 therapies, including intravenous ocrelizumab, subcutaneous ofatumumab, and intravenous ublituximab-xiiy. The study will assess imaging biomarkers, patient outcomes, safety, tolerability, and treatment satisfaction following the switch.

Why it matters
This trial directly targets market share held by rival anti-CD20 therapies Kesimpta (ofatumumab) and Briumvi (ublituximab-xiiy) by generating transition data to support switching patients to subcutaneous ocrelizumab.
