EDITOR'S TAKE
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Hoffmann-La Roche's completion of its pivotal Phase 3 trial evaluating
subcutaneous ocrelizumab marks the week's most consequential development,
positioning the market leader to defend its anti-CD20 franchise through a far
more convenient delivery route. This emphasis on subcutaneous optimization is
part of a broader industry push, mirrored by Sanofi initiating its own Phase 3
non-inferiority study to advance a subcutaneous formulation of frexalimab.
Concurrently, late-stage development for the BTK inhibitor class continues to
accelerate across both relapsing and progressive indications. Zenas BioPharma
has launched a Phase 3 trial for CNS-penetrable orelabrutinib in non-active
secondary progressive MS, while Roche's competing Phase 3 fenebrutinib study
against teriflunomide has closed to recruitment. Strategy leaders should watch
for Roche's imminent double-blind data disclosures, which will signal whether
SC convenience and BTK inhibition can successfully establish new standard-of-
care benchmarks.

1. Non-inferiority Study of Frexalimab Subcutaneous Administration Compared to
Intravenous Administration in Adult Participants With Multiple Sclerosis
   Impact 4/5 | ClinicalTrials.gov | 14 Aug 2026 | Phase 3 | Recruiting |
   Sanofi | NCT07325292

   Sanofi has initiated a Phase 3 open-label study (NCT07325292) evaluating
   the non-inferiority of subcutaneous (SC) frexalimab administered every 4
   weeks against intravenous (IV) frexalimab q4w. The trial is enrolling adult
   participants aged 18 to 60 with relapsing multiple sclerosis (RMS) and non-
   relapsing secondary progressive multiple sclerosis (nrSPMS). The primary
   treatment duration across Parts A and B is 48 weeks, followed by an
   optional extension in Part C.

   WHY IT MATTERS: Demonstrating non-inferiority of a monthly SC regimen
   against IV administration could optimize Sanofi's frexalimab delivery
   profile and enhance its competitive positioning against established MS
   therapies across both RMS and nrSPMS populations.
   https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT07325292

2. A Study of Orelabrutinib in Patients With Secondary Progressive Multiple
Sclerosis
   Impact 4/5 | ClinicalTrials.gov | 13 Aug 2026 | Phase 3 | Recruiting |
   Zenas BioPharma (USA), LLC | NCT07299019

   Zenas BioPharma has initiated recruitment for a global, randomized Phase 3
   trial (NCT07299019) evaluating orelabrutinib against placebo in
   approximately 990 patients with non-active secondary progressive multiple
   sclerosis. The study will assess the CNS-penetrable BTK inhibitor over a
   treatment period of 24 to 60 months, with subjects randomized in a 2:1
   ratio.

   WHY IT MATTERS: This trial advances another oral BTK inhibitor into pivotal
   development for non-active SPMS, threatening to establish a new disease-
   modifying standard in a high-unmet-need progressive population where
   current therapies are limited.
   https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT07299019

3. A Study to Evaluate the Efficacy and Safety of Fenebrutinib Compared With
Teriflunomide in Relapsing Multiple Sclerosis (RMS)
   Impact 4/5 | ClinicalTrials.gov | 13 Aug 2026 | Phase 3 | Active Not
   Recruiting | Hoffmann-La Roche | NCT04586010

   Hoffmann-La Roche's Phase 3 trial (NCT04586010) evaluating fenebrutinib
   against teriflunomide in adult participants with relapsing multiple
   sclerosis (RMS) is now active, not recruiting. The study compares the two
   agents 1:1 on primary endpoints of disability progression and relapse rate
   during a double-blind treatment phase. A potential open-label extension
   phase will be determined by Roche following the disclosure of the double-
   blind treatment results.

   WHY IT MATTERS: Completion of enrollment in this pivotal trial advances
   Roche's investigational BTK inhibitor fenebrutinib toward a head-to-head
   Phase 3 readout against Sanofi's Aubagio (teriflunomide) in the highly
   competitive RMS landscape.
   https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT04586010

4. A Phase III, Non-Inferiority, Randomized, Open-Label, Parallel Group,
Multicenter Study To Investigate The Pharmacokinetics, Pharmacodynamics,
Safety And Radiological And Clinical Effects Of Subcutaneous Ocrelizumab
Versus Intravenous Ocrelizumab In Patients With Multiple Sclerosis
   Impact 4/5 | ClinicalTrials.gov | 11 Aug 2026 | Phase 3 | Completed |
   Hoffmann-La Roche | NCT05232825

   Hoffmann-La Roche has completed the Phase 3 NCT05232825 trial evaluating
   subcutaneous (SC) ocrelizumab against its intravenous (IV) formulation in
   patients with relapsing multiple sclerosis (RMS) and primary progressive
   multiple sclerosis (PPMS). The multicenter, non-inferiority study assessed
   the SC formulation across pharmacokinetics, pharmacodynamics, safety,
   immunogenicity, and radiological and clinical endpoints. Completion of this
   pivotal study marks a critical clinical milestone in transitioning Roche's
   anti-CD20 franchise to a brief SC administration route.

   WHY IT MATTERS: A successful transition to a subcutaneous delivery method
   strengthens Roche's market dominance with Ocrevus by reducing
   administration time and burden, directly threatening rival anti-CD20
   therapies like Novartis's Kesimpta and TG Therapeutics' Briumvi.
   https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT05232825
