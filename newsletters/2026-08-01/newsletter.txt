EDITOR'S TAKE
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The battle for progressive MS is reaching a critical nexus as BTK inhibitors
aggressively target non-relapsing disease populations where traditional anti-
CD20s face efficacy limits. Zenas BioPharma launched dual Phase 3 trials for
its CNS-penetrable candidate orelabrutinib in primary and secondary
progressive MS, directly aligning with Novartis's Phase 3 initiation of
remibrutinib in SPMS. Concurrently, Roche is attempting to entrench its
franchise by completing recruitment for a higher-dose ocrelizumab regimen,
even as Amgen's biosimilar candidate ABP 692 moves closer to market entry.
These pipeline shifts coincide with structural commercial pressure on legacy
products, highlighted by Biogen's MS revenues being overtaken by non-MS assets
and pricing friction in Australia. Market observers should watch whether
upcoming Phase 3 readout timelines for oral BTK inhibitors validate their
disease-modifying potential in progressive populations while BMS tracks real-
world PML risks for Zeposia.

6. Risk of Progressive Multifocal Leukoencephalopathy Among Patients With
Multiple Sclerosis Exposed to Zeposia
   Impact 3/5 | ClinicalTrials.gov | 29 Jul 2026 | Observational | Recruiting
   | Bristol-Myers Squibb | NCT07732907

   Bristol Myers Squibb has initiated a post-marketing observational trial
   (NCT07732907) to evaluate the incidence and risk factors of progressive
   multifocal leukoencephalopathy (PML) in multiple sclerosis patients treated
   with Zeposia (ozanimod). The study will systematically collect reported PML
   cases to analyze patient characteristics and estimate occurrence rates. Key
   covariates under evaluation include age, exposure duration, prior
   immunosuppressant history, lymphopenia, and anti-JCV antibody status.

   WHY IT MATTERS: Real-world characterization of PML risk associated with
   Zeposia could impact its safety profile and prescriber preference relative
   to competing S1P receptor modulators and high-efficacy MS therapies.
   https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT07732907
