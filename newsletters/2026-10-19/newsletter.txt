EDITOR'S TAKE
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Phase 3 clinical trial initiations for Bruton's tyrosine kinase (BTK)
inhibitors in progressive multiple sclerosis took center stage this week,
signaling an aggressive push into populations with high unmet need. Zenas
BioPharma launched a global Phase 3 study evaluating orelabrutinib in primary
progressive MS, while Novartis initiated Phase 3 recruiting for remibrutinib
in secondary progressive MS. Alongside these BTK inhibitor advances, Sanofi
initiated a Phase 3 study evaluating a subcutaneous formulation of frexalimab,
reinforcing a broader market movement toward optimizing delivery methods and
novel oral or targeted mechanisms. Roche also maintained its lifecycle defense
of ocrelizumab with a Phase 2 bioequivalence study testing a new subcutaneous
formulation across RMS and PPMS cohorts. Moving forward, industry observers
should monitor recruitment momentum in these progressive MS studies to gauge
how rapidly next-generation oral and subcutaneous candidates can challenge
established parenteral standards.

1. Non-inferiority Study of Frexalimab Subcutaneous Administration Compared to
Intravenous Administration in Adult Participants With Multiple Sclerosis
   Impact 4/5 | ClinicalTrials.gov | 13 Oct 2026 | Phase 3 | Recruiting |
   Sanofi | NCT07325292

   Sanofi is recruiting for a randomized, open-label, 2-arm Phase 3 trial
   (NCT07325292) comparing subcutaneous (SC) to intravenous (IV)
   administration of frexalimab administered every 4 weeks in adult
   participants aged 18 to 60 with RMS and nrSPMS. The trial features a
   48-week study intervention duration across Parts A and B, followed by an
   optional Part C and a 6-month safety follow-up period for patients who
   discontinue treatment.

   WHY IT MATTERS: Sanofi's trial evaluates an every-4-week subcutaneous
   administration option for frexalimab, which could provide a more convenient
   dosing route relative to IV administration for RMS and nrSPMS patients.
   https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT07325292

2. A Study of Orelabrutinib in Patients With Primary Progressive Multiple
Sclerosis
   Impact 4/5 | ClinicalTrials.gov | 13 Oct 2026 | Phase 3 | Recruiting |
   Zenas BioPharma (USA), LLC | NCT07067463

   Zenas BioPharma has initiated a global Phase 3, randomized, double-blind
   study evaluating its CNS-penetrable BTK inhibitor, orelabrutinib, versus
   placebo in approximately 705 patients with primary progressive multiple
   sclerosis (PPMS). Patients will undergo 2:1 randomization (orelabrutinib to
   placebo) with treatment lasting between 30 and 60 months and a 12-month
   minimum duration.

   WHY IT MATTERS: The initiation of a Phase 3 trial in PPMS positions
   orelabrutinib as a direct future competitor to Roche's Ocrevus and other
   late-stage BTK inhibitors targeting progressive MS populations.
   https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT07067463

3. A Study to Evaluate the Efficacy and Safety of Remibrutinib in Secondary
Progressive Multiple Sclerosis
   Impact 4/5 | ClinicalTrials.gov | 13 Oct 2026 | Phase 3 | Recruiting |
   Novartis Pharmaceuticals | NCT07225504

   Novartis has initiated recruitment for a Phase 3 trial (NCT07225504)
   evaluating its BTK inhibitor, remibrutinib, in patients with secondary
   progressive multiple sclerosis (SPMS). The study is designed to generate
   efficacy and safety data for the asset in this progressive MS population.

   WHY IT MATTERS: The progression of remibrutinib into Phase 3 for SPMS
   advances Novartis's BTK inhibitor strategy into non-relapsing progressive
   disease, directly competing with late-stage oral pipeline assets from
   Sanofi and Roche.
   https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT07225504

4. A Study to Assess Bioequivalence of Two Subcutaneous (SC) Formulations of
Ocrelizumab in Participants With Multiple Sclerosis (MS)
   Impact 3/5 | ClinicalTrials.gov | 15 Oct 2026 | Phase 2 | Active Not
   Recruiting | Hoffmann-La Roche | NCT07074886

   Hoffmann-La Roche is conducting a Phase 2 trial (NCT07074886) evaluating
   the bioequivalence of a new subcutaneous (SC) test formulation of
   ocrelizumab compared to the marketed ocrelizumab SC reference formulation.
   The study is active but not recruiting and encompasses participants with
   relapsing multiple sclerosis (RMS) or primary progressive multiple
   sclerosis (PPMS). It features a controlled phase with a single dose of
   either the test or reference formulation, followed by a continuation phase
   where all participants receive the test formulation.

   WHY IT MATTERS: A successful bioequivalence readout allows Roche to
   optimize or update its subcutaneous ocrelizumab formulation, defending its
   high-efficacy franchise against competing anti-CD20 agents and subcutaneous
   therapies in RMS and PPMS.
   https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT07074886
